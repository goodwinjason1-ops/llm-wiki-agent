# trading to stay alive

### don't let the whims of the market dictate your risk

[![robot james's avatar](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!64tg!,w_36,h_36,c_fill,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fbucketeer-e05bbc84-baa3-437e-9518-adb32be77984.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fbe3e808a-4c78-431e-a4bc-92d71c8d0289_522x522.jpeg)](https://substack.com/@robotjames)

good day friends.

in light of recent market conditions, here’s a quick note about trading to keep your risk in check. so you can stay alive.

. . .

your risk is slippery.

you put a position on at a certain size.

your position gets bigger or smaller on you with the whims of the market

and sometimes the asset you’re trading starts moving a lot and so does your risk.

. . .

if you’re **long only investing for the long run**, it can be fine to just **buy things and leave them alone.**

this is reasonable because, if you are long an asset:

- your position gets bigger as you make more money

- your position gets smaller as you lose money


so your portfolio risk doesn’t really change except for changes in the volatility of the market _(and little boring things like uninvested dividends.)_

so it’s completely reasonable to buy the VT ETF, set dividends to reinvest just leave it alone forever.

. . .

but, if you are long **more than one asset**, some of them are going to go up more than others, and some are going to go down.

so, even if your _**total**_ portfolio risk stays kinda roughly the same, you’re going to be taking **more risk than you wanted in certain assets than other ones.**

so you probably wanna do some kind of rebalancing to:

- cut the positions that got bigger than you wanted

- add to the positions that got smaller.


you don’t want to do this too often cos trading costs money.

so you might decide to only do this every quarter or every year.

or, probably better, albeit a bit more time consuming, only do it when a position deviates outside of an acceptable range you have defined.

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!g3jT!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fd29203bf-9956-43d6-974b-492e0f52ee21_1236x578.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!g3jT!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2Fd29203bf-9956-43d6-974b-492e0f52ee21_1236x578.png)

**long only investing is the easy case here**. your position gets smaller as you lose money and your account gets smaller. so portfolio risk basically stays the same.

**in leveraged and short trades it gets hairier because these things aren’t true anymore**.

. . .

if you are shorting or trading on leverage you really need to **trade to stay alive.**

if you are short an asset that goes up, your portfolio risk gets bigger in two ways:

- the size (and riskiness) of the position increases

- you lost money and have less portfolio equity.


so you really need to be cutting size aggressively in shorts that go against you to stay alive.

. . .

if you’re trading on leverage, it’s a similar situation.

if you are levered long, and the thing goes down:

- the position gets a bit smaller

- your portfolio loses more money than that


so the position gets “bigger” in portfolio risk terms.

. . .

it’s worse if you’re levered short, and the thing goes up:

- your short position gets bigger

- your portfolio loses even more money.


so again the position gets bigger and riskier in portfolio terms.

kinda a triple whammy.

. . .

if you’re doing anything other than basic long only stuff then **trading to manage risk is non-negotiable.**

don’t think about whether there’s a “right time” to do it.

just do it. rebalancing positions is not edge.

you just have to do it to stay alive.
