one place that this can play out is in vix futures (and ETPs.)

vix futures are a lot of fun because:

- they are volatile and positively skewed

- customer positioning tends to be concentrated on one side, and sticky

- which can lead to tradeable inefficiencies. . . .


_. . . as we are about to see._

the vix complex, despite being relatively high profile, sure attracts a lot of dumb trading.

in particular, when really bad unexpected stuff happens, vix futures often trade too cheap.

there are a few reasons why this is the case but one of the most compelling is _**“idiots love systematically slamming vix futures esp on an uptick of vol.”**_

[![](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!sMKs!,w_1456,c_limit,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F5c464357-100f-4d07-9484-2384b4840277_1127x515.png)](https://substackcdn.com/image/fetch/$s_!sMKs!,f_auto,q_auto:good,fl_progressive:steep/https%3A%2F%2Fsubstack-post-media.s3.amazonaws.com%2Fpublic%2Fimages%2F5c464357-100f-4d07-9484-2384b4840277_1127x515.png)

we can quantify this by using spx options to determine whether vix futures look cheap or not. _(it’s all spx vol at the end of the day innit.)_
