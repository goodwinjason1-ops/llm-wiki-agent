# 5 Launch Posts - Used Car Buyer Kit

## Post 1 — “Don’t buy a lemon”

### Hook

Buying a used car in Australia? Don’t even inspect it until you ask for these 3 things.

### Script

Before you drive across town to look at a used car, ask the seller for:

1. the VIN,
2. the registration number,
3. service history photos.

Why?

The VIN lets you run a PPSR check for finance owing, stolen status, and written-off history. The rego helps you verify the car details. The service history tells you whether the odometer and maintenance story make sense.

If the seller refuses basic details, that’s already a red flag.

## Post 2 — PPSR reminder

### Hook

A $2 PPSR check can save you from a very expensive used-car mistake.

### Script

In Australia, a PPSR check can show whether a car has finance owing, has been reported stolen, or has written-off history.

You’ll usually need the VIN.

This does not replace a mechanic inspection, but it is one of the simplest checks to do before handing over money.

If a seller will not provide the VIN, slow down.

## Post 3 — Test-drive red flags

### Hook

Five red flags on a used-car test drive.

### Script

When test driving a used car, watch for:

1. steering pulling left or right,
2. vibration while braking,
3. gearbox clunks or slipping,
4. temperature rising above normal,
5. warning lights appearing during the drive.

One of these does not always mean “walk away”, but it does mean “get a qualified mechanic to inspect it before buying.”

## Post 4 — Seller pressure

### Hook

“If you don’t buy it today, someone else will.” Used-car buyer red flag?

### Script

Sometimes that’s true. Good cars sell quickly.

But pressure is also how buyers skip checks.

Before paying a deposit, make sure you’ve checked:

- VIN and rego,
- PPSR,
- service history,
- test drive notes,
- obvious leaks/warning lights,
- whether you need a mechanic inspection.

A genuine seller should understand reasonable checks.

## Post 5 — True cost calculator

### Hook

The asking price is not the real price of a used car.

### Script

Before negotiating, add up the immediate costs:

- transfer/stamp duty/rego,
- insurance,
- tyres,
- brakes,
- overdue service,
- battery,
- windscreen,
- inspection,
- known repairs.

A car listed at $12,000 might really be a $14,000 car if it needs tyres, brakes, rego, and a major service.

Use those costs to set your walk-away price before you negotiate.
