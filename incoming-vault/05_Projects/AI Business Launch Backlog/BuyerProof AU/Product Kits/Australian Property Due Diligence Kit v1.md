# Australian Property Due Diligence Kit

## Check before you commit

A practical preparation checklist, tracker, question script pack, and risk worksheet for Australians inspecting property, organising professional checks, and getting offer-ready.

## Who this is for

- first-home buyers,
- first investment property buyers,
- interstate or relocating buyers who can't inspect often,
- buyers not using a buyer's agent,
- buyers overwhelmed by contracts, finance dates, building and pest, strata, zoning, easements, flood/bushfire risk, or insurance.

## Important disclaimer

This kit is an educational preparation tool only. It is **not** legal, financial, investment, building, pest, insurance, tax, or property advice, and it does not tell you whether to buy a property. It cannot guarantee a property's condition, title, value, insurability, or suitability.

Always use qualified professionals: a conveyancer or solicitor for the contract and title, a licensed building inspector, a licensed pest inspector, a mortgage broker or lender for finance, an insurer for cover, and an accountant or tax adviser for investment questions.

Contracts, cooling-off periods, auction rules, disclosure requirements, zoning, and buying costs **vary by state/territory and by sale type**. Where this kit touches any of those, treat it as a prompt to **verify locally and professionally** — not as a statement of the rules.

## Official / professional checks to consider

Use current official sources for final checks. Good starting points:

- **Contract and title review** — a conveyancer or solicitor, engaged *before* you sign or bid. Title, easements, covenants, and caveats are checked through your state/territory land registry, usually via your conveyancer.
- **Building inspection** — a licensed/qualified building inspector.
- **Pest inspection** — a licensed/qualified pest (timber pest/termite) inspector; often combined with building.
- **Council/local authority** — zoning, planning overlays, approvals for past works, flood/bushfire overlays, and nearby development. Start with the council's website and your state/territory planning portal.
- **Strata/body corporate records search** (units, townhouses, some estates) — a strata search professional or your conveyancer.
- **Finance** — your lender or mortgage broker for pre-approval status, conditions, and dates.
- **Insurance** — an insurer for a quote *before* you commit; difficulty insuring is itself a signal to slow down.
- **Government guidance** — the Australian Government's Moneysmart website has general home-buying cost and process guidance; your state/territory consumer affairs / fair trading body publishes buying-property guides.
- **Internet** — check the address on the NBN Co website or with providers if connectivity matters to you.

# 0. Before you start

Prepare these before booking inspections or getting attached to a property:

- [ ] Budget range set with your lender/broker (pre-approval status and conditions understood — verify professionally)
- [ ] Deposit funds located and transfer timing understood
- [ ] Conveyancer/solicitor chosen (or shortlist ready) — ideally before you find "the one"
- [ ] Building and pest inspector shortlist for the target area
- [ ] Sale type understood: private treaty vs auction, and what that means for conditions and cooling-off **in your state/territory** — verify with your conveyancer
- [ ] Non-negotiables written down: location, size, condition, strata vs freestanding, maximum price
- [ ] This kit printed or open on your phone for inspection day
- [ ] A second person to attend inspections with you if possible

## Slow down before you start if

- you feel pressure to make an offer before any professional has looked at anything,
- your finance is "probably fine" but not confirmed in writing,
- you're planning to bid at auction without understanding what an unconditional purchase means in your state — verify professionally first.

## Immediate slow-down triggers

Any of these means: photograph it, ask about it, and make sure a professional looks at it before you commit.

- visible structural cracking or obvious floor slope,
- damp, mould, or water stains anywhere,
- strong odours or heavy air freshener/fresh paint that could be masking something,
- poor drainage or water pooling against the house,
- extensions/renovations that look unapproved — ask for approval documents,
- agent or vendor avoids direct answers or discourages inspections,
- signs of major repair without documentation,
- sale conditions pushing you to skip building/pest or contract review.

A slow-down trigger is not automatically a dealbreaker — it is a "get professional eyes on this before offering" flag.

## Questions to ask each professional

**Conveyancer/solicitor:** What conditions should I ask for in my situation? What do the title search and easements show? What are my key dates and what happens if I miss one? How does cooling-off work for this sale type in this state?

**Building/pest inspector:** Can you inspect before my offer deadline/auction? What areas couldn't you access? Which findings are urgent vs cosmetic? Roughly what class of cost is each major item — minor, significant, or get-quotes-first?

**Broker/lender:** Is my pre-approval current and what conditions remain? What happens if the valuation comes in low? What dates do you need to meet my finance condition?

**Insurer:** Can you quote this address before I commit? Are there flood/bushfire loadings or exclusions on this property?

# 5. Agent question scripts

Use these as prompts, not as a substitute for professional checks. Agents work for the vendor — polite, specific questions get better answers than vague ones. Follow up important answers by email so you have them in writing.

## Property history

- Why is the vendor selling?
- How long has the property been on the market? Has it been listed before?
- Have any offers been made or rejected? At what level did they fall over?
- Are there any defects or issues the vendor has disclosed?
- Has the property had insurance claims, flooding, storm damage, or major repairs?

## Renovations and approvals

- Have there been extensions or renovations? When?
- Are council approvals/permits/final certificates available for those works?
- Are there warranties or receipts for recent works?

## Strata/body corporate (if applicable)

- What are the current levies, and when were they last increased?
- Are there special levies current or proposed?
- Are there known building defects, disputes, or legal actions?
- How do I arrange access to the strata/body corporate records for a search?

## Sale process

- What sale method is being used, and what is the vendor's preferred settlement timing?
- Is there a contract available now for my conveyancer to review?
- Will offers before auction be considered (if auction)?
- What conditions has the vendor accepted or rejected before?

## Practical living

- What are the neighbours, noise, and parking like?
- What internet service is available at the address?
- Are there easements, shared access, or shared driveways?
- Are there rental or pet restrictions in the by-laws (if strata)?

## Email template — request contract and disclosures

> Hi [agent], I'm interested in [address]. Could you please send the contract of sale and any available reports/disclosures so my conveyancer can review them? Could you also confirm the sale method and any offer deadline? Thanks, [name]

# 6. Offer-readiness checklist

Before making an offer or registering to bid, confirm:

- [ ] Contract reviewed by my conveyancer/solicitor — not just skimmed by me
- [ ] Finance pre-approval current; remaining conditions understood with lender/broker
- [ ] Deposit funds available; amount, method, and timing confirmed
- [ ] Building and pest inspection done (or a professional plan for when it happens — verify what's possible for this sale type in your state)
- [ ] Strata search done, if applicable
- [ ] Insurance quote obtained for this address
- [ ] Key dates and conditions understood professionally (finance, inspections, settlement)
- [ ] Cooling-off position for this sale type and state verified with conveyancer — auctions are generally treated differently; verify locally
- [ ] Comparable sales reviewed; my price view written down
- [ ] Maximum price set **before** negotiation or auction — in writing
- [ ] Walk-away conditions written down (below)
- [ ] If auction: registration requirements and bidding rules for my state verified

## Walk-away conditions

Write these when calm, before negotiating. If one is triggered, that is your pre-agreed signal to stop and reassess.

| Condition | Why it matters | Professional to verify with |
|---|---|---|
| e.g. building report shows structural movement | repair cost unknown | building inspector + quotes |
| e.g. special levy proposed over $____ | changes true cost | strata search/conveyancer |
| e.g. price exceeds my written maximum of $____ | budget protection | broker/lender |
