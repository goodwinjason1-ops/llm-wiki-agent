## Post 1 — "Unknown is not a pass"

### Hook

Most property buyers don't fail a check before buying. They skip it.

### Script

Before going unconditional on a property, there's a standard list: contract review, building inspection, pest inspection, strata search, insurance quote, finance conditions, comparable sales.

The trap isn't getting a bad report. It's the checks that quietly never happen because the auction was Saturday.

## Post 2 — The two-week scramble

### Hook

You get about two weeks to organise the biggest purchase of your life. Here's the order that stops the scramble.

### Script

The mistake: fall in love with a property first, then scramble to find a conveyancer, book building and pest, sort finance conditions, and get an insurance quote before the deadline.

The fix: line these up **before** you find "the one" —

1. finance pre-approval status confirmed with your broker/lender,
2. conveyancer/solicitor chosen,
3. building and pest inspector shortlist for your target area,
4. your maximum price and non-negotiables written down.

Then when the right property appears, you're executing a plan instead of improvising one.

## Post 3 — Inspection-day slow-down triggers

### Hook

Five things at an open home that mean "slow down" — not "walk away", just "slow down".

### Script

You're not a building inspector, but you can spot what's worth professional eyes:

1. cracks in walls — photograph them, note where and how wide,
2. damp smell, mould, or water stains — anywhere,
3. heavy air freshener or fresh paint in one spot,
4. water pooling against the house after rain,
5. an extension that nobody can produce approval documents for.

None of these automatically kills a deal. Each one means: get it in front of a licensed building/pest inspector and your conveyancer before you commit.

## Post 4 — The asking price is not the price

### Hook

A $750,000 property is not a $750,000 decision.

### Script

Before you set your maximum price, add up what lands on top:

- stamp/transfer duty (varies by state — check your state revenue office's calculator, and check what concessions you might be eligible for),
- conveyancing fees,
- building and pest inspections,
- lender fees, and lenders mortgage insurance if it applies to you — ask your broker,
- insurance,
- strata levies, rates, water,
- immediate repairs from the inspection reports,
- moving costs and a buffer.

Work that out **before** the negotiation or auction, and your maximum price becomes a real number instead of a feeling.

## Post 5 — Walk-away conditions

### Hook

Decide when you'd walk away from a property before you fall in love with it.

### Script

Auctions and hot negotiations are designed to make you decide fast. The counter-move is boring and powerful: written walk-away conditions.

Before you offer or bid, write down things like:

- "If the building report shows structural movement and I can't get quotes in time…"
- "If the strata search shows a special levy over $X…"
- "If the price passes my written maximum of $Y…"

…then the decision is already made — by the calm version of you, not the one bidding under pressure.

This isn't advice on what to buy. It's a way to make sure the professionals' findings actually get used.
