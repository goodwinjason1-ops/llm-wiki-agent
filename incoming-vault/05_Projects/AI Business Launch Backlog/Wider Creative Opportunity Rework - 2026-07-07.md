# Wider Creative Opportunity Rework

## Why the previous target was weak

The mechanism — turning existing knowledge/content/assets into monetizable products, templates, systems, and funnels — is still strong.

The weak part was starting with **AI/business YouTubers** or generic **small business coaches**.

Problems:

- They are already AI-aware.
- They are exposed to these offers constantly.
- They may already have teams, funnels, products, or strong opinions.
- The competition for their attention is high.
- The offer sounds less magical to them.

The better target is people/businesses with valuable expertise, proof, or visual work, but poor packaging and low AI sophistication.

## Revised thesis

> Build a one-month productized service that takes messy real-world expertise from non-tech, low-AI-adoption niches and turns it into a sellable asset: templates, checklists, guides, calculators, mini-courses, quote packs, training manuals, or lead magnets.

This should start as a service, not SaaS.

## Best revised opportunity

# Local Expertise → Sellable Template Pack Studio

## One-line offer

> “I turn the way you already do your work into a sellable template pack, checklist, guide, or mini-course — so customers can buy your knowledge even when you’re not available.”

## Better target customers

Target less-AI-aware, practical experts and small operators:

| Target | Existing asset | Product we create | Why they buy |
|---|---|---|---|
| Experienced tradies | Job photos, quoting experience, common customer questions | Quote checklist, maintenance guide, homeowner prep pack | Generates leads and builds trust |
| Wedding/event vendors | Run sheets, vendor checklists, planning knowledge | Event planning template pack | Couples pay for planning help |
| Dog trainers/groomers | Behaviour routines, owner instructions | Puppy/new-dog starter kit | Repetitive advice becomes product |
| Driving instructors | Lesson structure, test tips | Learner driver logbook/test prep kit | Parents/students need structure |
| Cleaners/organisers | Cleaning systems, checklists | Bond-clean/pre-sale prep kit | Clear practical outcome |
| Gardeners/landscapers | Seasonal knowledge, maintenance plans | Seasonal garden care calendar | Local homeowners need timing help |
| Tutors/music teachers | Lesson plans, exercises | Practice packs, exam prep templates | Parents buy outcomes |
| NDIS/allied health admin-adjacent providers | Intake/process knowledge | Non-clinical onboarding/admin packs | Must avoid medical advice; admin only |
| Property managers/buyer advocates | Inspection routines, suburb checks | Inspection checklist + offer prep pack | High-value decisions |
| Mechanics/detailers | Maintenance checklists, buyer checks | Used-car buyer inspection kit | Easy consumer pain point |

## Why this is more creative and less crowded

Most AI agencies are chasing:

- AI receptionists,
- chatbots,
- generic lead gen,
- AI content repurposing,
- coaches/creators.

This targets **offline expertise trapped in people’s heads**.

The product is not “AI.” The product is:

- more leads,
- authority,
- passive-ish product revenue,
- better onboarding,
- fewer repeated explanations,
- a professional sales asset.

AI is just our production advantage.

## Strongest first niche options

### Option A — Homeowner/Tradie Knowledge Products

Examples:

- “Pre-Renovation Planning Checklist” for builders/carpenters.
- “Before You Call a Plumber” homeowner guide.
- “Bathroom Renovation Readiness Kit.”
- “Rental Property Maintenance Checklist.”
- “Storm Season Home Prep Guide.”

Pros:

- Connects to Jayse’s local tradie idea without competing with AI receptionist tools.
- Tradies often have photos and real-world tips.
- Can become lead magnets for their actual services.

Cons:

- Tradies may not care about selling low-ticket products directly; sell it as lead-gen/sales asset.

Best angle:

> “I’ll create a homeowner guide/checklist from your expertise that helps customers trust you before they call.”

### Option B — Wedding/Event Vendor Template Packs

Examples:

- wedding run sheet,
- vendor contact sheet,
- budget tracker,
- photography shot list,
- emergency day checklist,
- MC/reception planning pack.

Pros:

- Buyers already purchase digital templates.
- Vendors have repeated advice and portfolio photos.
- Visual product is easy to demonstrate.

Cons:

- Etsy/Canva template market can be crowded; local/vendor-specific angle helps.

Best angle:

> “I’ll turn your event process into a branded planning pack you can sell or give away to book more clients.”

### Option C — Used-Car Buyer / Mechanic Inspection Kits

Examples:

- used-car inspection checklist,
- pre-purchase message scripts,
- negotiation checklist,
- service-history review guide,
- red-flags guide,
- calculator for running costs.

Pros:

- High consumer pain.
- Mechanics/detailers/car enthusiasts may have expertise but no digital product.
- Could be sold direct-to-consumer later.

Cons:

- Need accuracy and disclaimers; avoid pretending to replace mechanic inspections.

Best angle:

> “I’ll turn your inspection know-how into a buyer checklist that brings you pre-purchase inspection leads.”

### Option D — Cleaning / Home Organisation Packs

Examples:

- bond-clean checklist,
- pre-sale home prep checklist,
- Airbnb turnover SOP,
- weekly cleaning tracker,
- declutter challenge workbook.

Pros:

- Very practical.
- Low regulation.
- Easy to make and sell visually.

Cons:

- Digital template competition; local service tie-in needed.

Best angle:

> “I’ll make a branded checklist pack that gets your cleaning/organising business more enquiries and saves you repeating instructions.”

## Revised top pick

# Before/After Sales Kit for Home-Service Businesses

This is more grounded than “sell digital products to YouTubers” and less crowded than “AI receptionist.”

## One-line offer

> “I turn your job photos, customer FAQs, and know-how into a quote-winning sales kit: case studies, homeowner checklist, quote follow-up messages, and a simple landing page.”

## Why this may be the best practical first move

- We can sell locally.
- Buyer does not need to understand AI.
- It creates immediate business value: better trust and higher quote conversion.
- It avoids crowded call-answering software.
- It is fast to deliver.
- It can later add digital products, lead magnets, content, and automations.

## Deliverables

### Starter: Quote-Winning Sales Kit

Price: **A$500–A$1,500**

Deliverables:

1. One-page case-study landing page.
2. 3 before/after job stories from existing photos.
3. Homeowner prep checklist / buyer guide.
4. Quote follow-up SMS/email sequence.
5. FAQ section from owner interview.
6. 5 social posts from the project photos.
7. Optional simple lead magnet PDF.

## First target niches

| Rank | Niche | Why |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Bathroom/kitchen renovators | Visual proof, high-ticket quotes, trust matters |
| 2 | Landscapers/garden makeovers | Before/after photos, seasonal guides |
| 3 | Roofers/gutter/storm prep | Urgency + homeowner education |
| 4 | Painters/plasterers | Visual proof, simple transformations |
| 5 | Detailers/mechanics | Before/after and buyer education |
| 6 | Cleaners/organisers | Checklists and repeatable systems |

## 30-day plan

### Week 1 — Build sample sales kit

- Pick one niche: **bathroom renovators/landscapers/detailers**.
- Create fictional/sample before-after project.
- Build:
  - landing page copy,
  - case study layout,
  - homeowner checklist,
  - quote follow-up sequence,
  - social posts.
- Make a 2-minute demo video.

### Week 2 — Local prospecting

- Build list of 50 local businesses with poor websites but good photos/reviews.
- Send personalized messages showing one specific improvement.
- Offer 3 free mini-audits.

### Week 3 — First pilot

- Sell/complete one discounted sales kit.
- Use their real photos/reviews/FAQs.
- Create case study with permission.

### Week 4 — Package/refine

- Turn delivery into a repeatable checklist.
- Raise price.
- Outreach to 100 similar businesses.
- Add optional monthly content/reporting retainer.

## Pricing path

| Stage | Price |
|---|---:|
| First pilot | A$250–A$500 |
| Standard sales kit | A$750–A$1,500 |
| Premium kit + simple site | A$1,500–A$3,000 |
| Monthly content/follow-up retainer | A$300–A$900/mo |

## Why this may beat pure digital-product factory

Instead of asking a creator to care about a product funnel, we ask a local business:

> “Do your photos and quotes actually help you win jobs?”

That is more concrete.

We can still create digital products/lead magnets, but they are embedded in a bigger business outcome: **win more quotes**.

- weak websites,
- decent job value,

## Decision checkpoint after 30 days

Continue if:

- 50 businesses contacted,
- 5 audits delivered,
- 2+ serious conversations,
- 1 paid pilot,
- delivery time under 8 hours after templates,
- prospect agrees the asset helps win quotes.

If not, pivot the same assets into a direct-to-consumer template pack: homeowner renovation checklist, used-car buyer kit, bond-clean checklist, etc.
